title: "Visits, clicks and conversions"
description: "ClickPattern records three kinds of events for every visitor: a visit when they open your campaign URL, a click when they go from a lander to an offer, and a conversion when the affiliate network or store reports a sale or lead. A unique click ID ties them together."

### The event model

| Event | When it's recorded | Report column |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visit | A visitor opens your campaign URL (or a page with your direct tracking script). | **Visits**, **Unique Visits** |
| Click | The visitor clicks a CTA on your lander and is sent to an offer. | **Clicks**, **Unique Clicks** |
| Conversion | A postback, pixel or store integration reports a sale, lead or other event for that visitor's click ID. | **Conversions** |
| Refund | A conversion is reversed. | Lowers revenue and profit |

If a campaign sends visitors straight to an offer (no lander), the visit also counts as the click. You don't need a lander for Clicks to fill in.

### The click ID

Every visit gets a click ID, a UUID such as `7d2f4a10-9c3e-4b8a-a1f2-5e6d7c8b9a01`. ClickPattern puts it in your offer URL wherever you place the `{clickid}` token. Your affiliate network stores it and sends it back in the postback, and that is how a conversion is matched to the visit, campaign, traffic source and ad.

Without `{clickid}` in the offer URL, a postback has nothing to match on and the conversion can't be attributed.

ClickPattern also captures the ad platform's own click ID when it is in the URL, so you don't have to map it yourself. These are read automatically: `fbclid`, `gclid`, `gbraid`, `wbraid`, `ttclid`, `twclid`, `li_fat_id`, `epik`, `msclkid`, `ScCid`, `dclid` and `oppref`.

### Unique vs non-unique visits

Every visit is recorded, including refreshes and repeat visits. ClickPattern then marks each one as unique or non-unique, per campaign.

| How the visitor is recognized | Window |
| --- | --- |
| ClickPattern visitor cookie (set on the first visit) | A repeat visit within 24 hours of the first is non-unique. |
| No cookie (blocked or cleared), so a fingerprint of IP address and browser details is used | A repeat visit within 1 hour is non-unique. |

The fingerprint window is shorter because many people can share one IP address and browser version, for example in an office. A visitor who returns after the window, or who loses the cookie and returns after an hour, counts as unique again. Uniqueness is tracked separately for visits and for lander clicks.

### Refunds are separate negative events

ClickPattern never edits the original conversion's numbers. A refund is recorded as its own event with a negative payout, linked to the original conversion. The conversion still counts in **Conversions**, while revenue and profit go down by the refunded amount. Partial refunds work the same way.

A refund is created by:

- A postback with `status=refund`.
- A refund or cancellation from a connected store such as Shopify.

A later postback with `status=rejected` does not reverse a conversion. Only an explicit refund does.

If the campaign's cost model is CPA or RevShare, a refund also reverses the cost of that conversion. CPC and CPM costs are not reversed, because the click or impression still happened.

### Click data lookup window

ClickPattern keeps click data for 365 days. A conversion can be attributed as long as its click ID is still on file, so in practice the attribution window is about one year. A postback for an older click is refused as an unknown click.

### FAQ

**Why do I have more visits than unique visits?** Repeat visits and page refreshes are recorded but marked non-unique. Compare the two columns to see how much repeat traffic a source sends.

**Does a paused campaign still record conversions?** Yes. Pausing stops new visits, but conversions for clicks made earlier are still accepted and attributed.

**Why don't I see clicks on a campaign without a lander?** You should. With no lander, each visit is counted as both a visit and a click.
